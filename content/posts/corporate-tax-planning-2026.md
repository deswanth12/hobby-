# Corporate Tax Planning and Assessment Strategies under Income Tax Act, 1961: Key Protocols for FY 2025-26

Navigating the Indian corporate taxation regime requires a balance between statutory compliance and forward-looking fiscal planning. In recent assessment years, the **Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT)** has intensified data triangulation across the **Annual Information Statement (AIS)**, **Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS)**, and **GST Form GSTR-2B/3B**, eliminating the scope for discrepancies in corporate tax reporting.

This advisory commentary examines critical considerations under the concessional tax regime (Section 115BAA), optimization of Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT), and institutional frameworks for faceless assessment defenses.

---

## 1. Concessional Tax Regime Evaluation: Section 115BAA vs. Normal Provisions

Domestic corporate entities face a critical structural decision between electing the concessional tax regime under **Section 115BAA** (effective tax rate of 25.168% inclusive of 10% surcharge and 4% cess) or continuing under the standard corporate tax slab of 30% (or 25% for companies with turnover up to ₹400 Crore in the base year).

### Comparative Evaluation Parameters:

| Evaluation Dimension | Standard Regime (Sec 115JB Applicable) | Concessional Regime (Sec 115BAA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Base Tax Rate** | 25% / 30% + Surcharge + Cess | 22% flat + 10% Surcharge + 4% Cess (25.168%) |
| **MAT Liability (Sec 115JB)** | Applicable at 15% on Book Profits | Completely Exempt from MAT |
| **Brought-Forward MAT Credit** | Eligible for set-off under Sec 115JAA | Lapses permanently upon opting into 115BAA |
| **Accelerated Depreciation** | Permitted under Section 32 | Disallowed (Standard normal depreciation only) |
| **Section 80-IA / 80-IB Deductions** | Available subject to conditions | Completely foregone |
| **Irrevocability** | Can switch into 115BAA in any future year | Irrevocable once Form 10-IC is submitted |

> **Key Advisory Rule**: An enterprise with substantial accumulated MAT credit balances should model its taxable cash flows over a 3-to-5-year horizon. Forfeiting accumulated MAT credit by prematurely filing Form 10-IC can result in permanent loss of accrued tax assets.

## 2. Faceless Assessment & Scrutiny Defense Architecture

Under the **Faceless Assessment Scheme (Section 144B)**, assessment proceedings are conducted through the **National Faceless Assessment Centre (NaFAC)**. Written submissions must adhere to strict evidentiary thresholds:

1. **Reconciliation Schedules**: Every response to a Section 142(1) notice must include a reconciliation statement between the audited financial statements, Form 26AS, AIS/TIS data, and the ITR computation.
2. **Substantiation of Expenses (Section 37(1))**: Commercial expediency must be demonstrated through contemporaneous contractual agreements, invoices, proof of delivery, and banking transaction logs.
3. **Cash Flow & Unexplained Credits (Section 68)**: For share capital, premium, or unsecured borrowings, the three-fold statutory burden remains on the taxpayer: identity of the creditor, creditworthiness, and genuineness of the transaction.

---

## 3. Advance Tax Compliance Timelines

To avert mandatory compensatory interest under **Section 234B** and **Section 234C**, corporate taxpayers must adhere to the quarterly advance tax payment milestones:

- **15th June**: Not less than 15% of estimated net tax liability.
- **15th September**: Not less than 45% of estimated net tax liability.
- **15th December**: Not less than 75% of estimated net tax liability.
- **15th March**: 100% of estimated net tax liability.
