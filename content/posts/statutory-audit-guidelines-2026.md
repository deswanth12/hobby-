# Statutory Audit Guidelines under Companies Act 2013: Key Auditor Obligations and Reporting Norms for FY 2025-26

The statutory audit of a corporate entity is the cornerstone of regulatory compliance, investor protection, and financial integrity within the Indian corporate ecosystem. Governed by the **Companies Act, 2013** and administered under the regulatory oversight of the **Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA)** and the **National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA)**, statutory audit obligations require rigorous technical execution, complete independence, and adherence to the **Standards on Auditing (SAs)** issued by the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI)**.

This regulatory analysis outlines the statutory framework, reporting obligations under the **Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 (CARO 2020)**, and the evaluation of **Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting (IFCoFR)**.

---

## 1. Statutory Foundation & Legislative Mandate

Under **Section 139** of the Companies Act, 2013, every company incorporated in India—regardless of its paid-up capital, annual turnover, or operational scale—must appoint an independent practicing Chartered Accountant or a firm of Chartered Accountants as its statutory auditor. 

The primary obligations of the statutory auditor are codified in **Section 143**:

1. **Inquiry Obligations (Section 143(1))**: The auditor is mandated to inquire into specific commercial matters, including whether loans and advances made on the basis of security have been properly secured, whether book entries are prejudicial to the interests of the company, and whether personal expenses have been debited to revenue accounts.
2. **True and Fair Opinion (Section 143(2))**: The auditor must report to the members of the company on the financial statements examined, stating whether the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, and cash flow statement exhibit a true and fair view of the state of the company's affairs.
3. **Statutory Matters to be Stated (Section 143(3))**: Specific statutory disclosures regarding proper maintenance of books of account, compliance with accounting standards (AS/Ind AS), adequacy of internal financial controls, and disqualifications of directors under Section 164(2).

## 2. Applicability Across Corporate Categories

The statutory audit mandate applies universally, but specific reporting tiers depend on entity size and public interest exposure:

| Corporate Category | Statutory Audit Mandate | CARO 2020 Applicability | IFC Reporting Obligation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Public Limited Companies** | Mandatory for all entities | Mandatory without exception | Mandatory under Sec 143(3)(i) |
| **Private Limited Companies (Large)** | Mandatory for all entities | Applicable if Turnover > ₹50 Cr or Borrowings > ₹10 Cr | Mandatory |
| **Small Companies (Sec 2(85))** | Mandatory | Explicitly Exempt | Exempted via MCA Notification |
| **One Person Companies (OPCs)** | Mandatory | Explicitly Exempt | Exempted via MCA Notification |
| **Section 8 Companies (Non-Profit)**| Mandatory | Explicitly Exempt | Exempted if criteria met |

*Note: As defined under Section 2(85), a Small Company has paid-up share capital not exceeding ₹4 Crore and turnover not exceeding ₹40 Crore.*

## 3. Comprehensive Reporting under CARO 2020

The **Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020** substantially expanded the auditor's diligence obligations. Key focus areas include:

### Clause (i): Property, Plant and Equipment and Intangible Assets
- Verification of whether the company maintains proper records showing full particulars, including quantitative details and situation of Property, Plant and Equipment.
- Verification of physical inventory and confirmation whether any material discrepancies were noticed on physical verification.
- Specific reporting on whether proceedings have been initiated or are pending against the company for holding any **benami property** under the Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act, 1988.

### Clause (ii): Inventory and Working Capital Limits
- Regularity and coverage of physical verification of inventory by management (coverage >= 10% threshold).
- Crucial reconciliation: Where the company has been sanctioned working capital limits exceeding **₹5 Crore** from banks or financial institutions on the basis of security of current assets, the auditor must verify whether the quarterly returns or statements filed with banks agree with the books of account.

### Clause (vii): Statutory Dues Regularity
- Verification of undisputed statutory dues including Goods and Services Tax (GST), Provident Fund (PF), Employees' State Insurance (ESI), Income Tax, and Customs Duty.
- Explicit disclosure of undisputed amounts payable in respect of statutory dues outstanding for more than **six months** from the date they became payable.

### Clause (xix): Material Uncertainty regarding Going Concern
- Evaluation of the company's financial ratios, ageing and expected dates of realization of financial assets, payment of financial liabilities, and management plans.
- Formal statement whether any material uncertainty exists as to whether the company is capable of meeting its liabilities existing at the date of balance sheet as and when they fall due within a period of one year.

## 4. Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting (IFCoFR)

Under **Section 143(3)(i)** of the Act, the auditor must report whether the company has adequate internal financial controls with reference to financial statements in place and the operating effectiveness of such controls.

The evaluation process involves a structured three-tier assessment:

1. **Entity-Level Controls (ELC)**: Governance tone at the top, human resource policies, anti-fraud whistle-blower mechanisms, and board oversight.
2. **Process-Level Controls (PLC)**: Segregation of duties, approval hierarchies in Procure-to-Pay (P2P), Order-to-Cash (O2C), and Hire-to-Retire (H2R) workflows.
3. **General IT Controls (GITC)**: User access management, change management controls, password complexity policies, and system backup integrity.

---

## 5. Auditor Documentation & ICAI Standards on Auditing (SAs)

In terms of **SA 230 (Audit Documentation)**, the auditor must assemble audit documentation in an audit file on a timely basis after the date of the auditor's report. The retention period for audit files under ICAI norms is a minimum of **seven years** from the date of the auditor's report.

Key Standards on Auditing applied during the statutory engagement include:

- **SA 240**: The Auditor's Responsibilities Relating to Fraud in an Audit of Financial Statements.
- **SA 315**: Identifying and Assessing the Risks of Material Misstatement Through Understanding the Entity and Its Environment.
- **SA 500**: Audit Evidence — obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
- **SA 570 (Revised)**: Going Concern obligations and management representation evaluation.
- **SA 700 (Revised)**: Forming an Opinion and Reporting on Financial Statements.

## 6. Auditor Responsibility vs. Management Responsibility

A foundational principle of statutory assurance is the separation of responsibilities:

| Operational Dimension | Responsibility of Management | Responsibility of Statutory Auditor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Preparation of Accounts** | Primary responsibility for preparing true and fair financial statements. | Objective examination and expression of independent statutory opinion. |
| **Internal Controls** | Designing, implementing, and maintaining effective financial controls. | Testing design and operating effectiveness of controls over financial reporting. |
| **Fraud Prevention** | Implementing safeguards to prevent, detect, and investigate enterprise fraud. | Assessing risk of material misstatement resulting from fraud under SA 240. |
| **Going Concern** | Assessing the entity's ability to continue operations for the foreseeable future. | Evaluating appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis. |
