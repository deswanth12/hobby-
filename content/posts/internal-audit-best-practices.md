# Internal Audit Framework and Operational Risk Appraisal: Practical Control Architecture for Growing Enterprises

*DRAFT WORKING PAPER — STRICTLY FOR INTERNAL COMMITTEE REVIEW & STATUTORY PLANNING*

Internal audit has evolved from a historical transaction-checking mechanism into an active instrument of executive risk management, internal financial control (IFC) testing, and operational value preservation. While statutory audits provide external stakeholders with independent assurance regarding financial statements, the internal audit function acts as an internal diagnostic arm for the Board of Directors, the Audit Committee, and operational management.

This working document examines the legislative mandate under **Section 138** of the **Companies Act, 2013**, the structure of a **Risk-Based Internal Audit (RBIA)** universe, core business cycles, and cost-control integration through the lens of dual Chartered Accountancy (FCA) and Cost & Management Accountancy (CMA) governance.

---

## 1. Statutory Mandate under Section 138 of Companies Act, 2013

Under **Section 138** read with **Rule 13** of the **Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014**, the following classes of companies are legally required to appoint an internal auditor:

| Category of Entity | Paid-Up Share Capital | Annual Turnover | Outstanding Loans/Borrowings | Outstanding Deposits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Listed Companies** | Mandatory for all listed entities without monetary threshold limits | Mandatory | Mandatory | Mandatory |
| **Unlisted Public Companies** | >= ₹50 Crore in preceding FY | >= ₹200 Crore in preceding FY | > ₹100 Crore at any point in preceding FY | >= ₹25 Crore at any point in preceding FY |
| **Private Limited Companies** | Not Applicable | >= ₹200 Crore in preceding FY | > ₹100 Crore at any point in preceding FY | Not Applicable |

*The internal auditor may be either a Chartered Accountant, a Cost Accountant, or such other professional as may be decided by the Board.*

## 2. Structural Comparison: Statutory Audit vs. Internal Audit

Understanding the distinct jurisdictional boundaries between statutory assurance and internal audit is vital for enterprise leadership:

| Practice Dimension | Statutory Audit (Sec 139 / 143) | Internal Audit (Sec 138) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Beneficiary** | Shareholders, Lenders, Regulators, Public | Board of Directors, Audit Committee, CEO/CFO |
| **Scope of Work** | True and fair view of financial statements | Operational efficiency, fraud prevention, process gaps |
| **Reporting Authority** | Directly to Members/Shareholders in AGM | Audit Committee / Board of Directors |
| **Standards Applied** | Standards on Auditing (SAs) issued by ICAI | Standards on Internal Audit (SIAs) issued by ICAI |
| **Frequency** | Annual engagement with quarterly reviews | Continuous or quarterly phased evaluation |
| **Remediation Focus** | Reporting misstatements & qualification | Process redesign & mitigation recommendations |

## 3. Designing a Risk-Based Internal Audit (RBIA) Framework

A robust internal audit program is structured around a risk-scoring matrix evaluating both **Likelihood of Occurrence** and **Magnitude of Impact**:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                         RISK IDENTIFICATION MATRIX                             |
|                                                                                |
|   IMPACT ^                                                                     |
|   High   |  [Medium Risk]            [CRITICAL / HIGH RISK]                    |
|          |  (Mitigate & Monitor)     (Immediate Board Attention & Audit)       |
|          |                                                                     |
|   Low    |  [Low Risk]               [Medium Risk]                             |
|          |  (Accept & Periodic Check)(Internal Process Review)                 |
|          +-------------------------------------------------------->            |
|             Low                      High                 LIKELIHOOD           |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Core Audit Phases:
1. **Universe Mapping**: Documenting all operational units, business units, subsidiaries, and statutory compliance calendars.
2. **Inherent Risk Evaluation**: Evaluating operational complexity, transaction volumes, cash exposure, and regulatory exposure without taking controls into account.
3. **Control Design Evaluation (Walkthroughs)**: Testing whether established Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) adequately mitigate the identified risks.
4. **Substantive Operating Effectiveness Testing**: Sample verification of actual transactions to confirm controls operate as designed throughout the financial year.
5. **Issue Escalation & Tracking**: Formal audit observations accompanied by management responses, agreed action plans, designated owners, and completion timelines.

## 4. Key Operational Cycles & Control Testing

### Cycle A: Procure-to-Pay (P2P)
- **Purchase Requisitions**: Verify automated three-way matching between Purchase Order (PO), Goods Receipt Note (GRN), and Vendor Invoice.
- **Vendor Master Governance**: Periodic review of vendor bank details, PAN/GSTIN validation, and dual-authorization for any changes to vendor master records to prevent phantom vendor fraud.
- **Ageing Analysis of Creditors**: Identification of disputed balances and adherence to MSMED Act 45-day payment statutory deadlines.

### Cycle B: Order-to-Cash (O2C)
- **Credit Limit Authorization**: Ensure sales dispatches strictly comply with approved customer credit thresholds.
- **Billing Integrity**: Reconciliation between physical gate passes, e-Way bills, e-Invoices, and accounts receivable general ledger.
- **Debtors Ageing & Write-off Policy**: Formal Board/CFO approval verification for discounts, write-offs, and bad debt provisioning.

### Cycle C: Treasury & Cash Management
- **Dual Signatory Enforcement**: Verification of banking mandate enforcement for high-value fund transfers.
- **Monthly Bank Reconciliations**: Detailed scrutiny of un-cleared cheques and stale deposits older than 30 days.

---

## 5. Integrating Cost Management (CMA Dimension)

Combining Chartered Accountancy compliance with Cost & Management Accountancy provides unique governance advantages:

1. **Unit-Cost Breakdown**: Verifying standard costing versus actual costing variances across material, labor, and factory overheads.
2. **Scrap & Yield Analysis**: Reviewing theoretical manufacturing input-output ratios against physical recovery percentages to detect unrecorded leakage.
3. **Working Capital Cycle Optimization**: Analyzing Days Sales Outstanding (DSO), Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO), and Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) to release trapped cash flow.

---

## 6. Audit Committee Reporting Architecture

Internal audit reports must be actionable and concise. Each audit finding must incorporate four standard elements:
- **Condition**: What was observed during testing (the factual evidence).
- **Criteria**: What standard or policy was breached (the SOP or statute).
- **Cause**: Why the failure occurred (system limitation, lack of training, absence of segregation).
- **Consequence / Risk**: The financial, operational, or legal impact on the business.
- **Recommendation & Remediating Action**: Specific corrective steps with agreed deadlines.
